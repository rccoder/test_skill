# Healthy Eating

## Overview

Healthy eating is about consuming a variety of nutrient-dense foods in the right proportions to support energy, immunity, and long-term health. The core principle is **balance, variety, and moderation** — no single food or food group provides everything the body needs.

## When to Use

- Planning daily or weekly meals
- Choosing what to eat at a restaurant or grocery store
- Seeking general nutrition advice (not medical dietary treatment)
- Building habits for sustained energy and health
- Evaluating whether a diet is balanced

**Not for:** medical conditions requiring a prescribed diet (diabetes, celiac, allergies, etc.) — refer to a registered dietitian or doctor.

## Core Principles

1. **Variety** — eat across all food groups; no single food covers all nutrients.
2. **Balance** — match portions to activity level and energy needs.
3. **Moderation** — enjoy all foods; avoid extremes of restriction or overconsumption.
4. **Whole over processed** — prefer minimally processed foods closer to their natural state.
5. **Hydration** — water is the default beverage; limit sugary drinks.

## Macronutrient Targets (general adult)

| Macronutrient | Share of daily calories | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | 45–65% | Whole grains, fruits, vegetables, legumes |
| Protein | 10–35% | Fish, poultry, eggs, legumes, nuts, dairy |
| Fat | 20–35% | Avocado, nuts, seeds, olive oil, fatty fish |

**Quality matters more than ratios:** choose complex carbs over refined sugars, unsaturated fats over trans fats, and lean/plant proteins over highly processed meats.

## The Healthy Plate Model

Divide a standard plate as follows:

- **½ plate** — non-starchy vegetables (leafy greens, broccoli, peppers, carrots)
- **¼ plate** — whole grains or starchy vegetables (brown rice, quinoa, sweet potato, oats)
- **¼ plate** — protein (fish, chicken, tofu, beans, eggs)
- **Side** — fruit and a small amount of healthy fat

## Quick Reference

| Goal | Action |
|---|---|
| More fiber | Add a vegetable or fruit to every meal |
| Stable energy | Pair carbs with protein + fat |
| Less sugar | Replace soda with water / unsweetened tea |
| Better fats | Cook with olive oil; snack on nuts |
| More protein | Include a palm-sized portion at each meal |

## Meal Planning Checklist

- [ ] Includes vegetables or fruit at every meal
- [ ] Has a protein source at every meal
- [ ] Uses whole grains for at least half of carb intake
- [ ] Limits ultra-processed foods to occasional treats
- [ ] Stays hydrated (≈1.5–2 L water/day, adjusted for activity/climate)
- [ ] Leaves room for enjoyable foods — sustainability > perfection

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Cutting entire food groups | Keep variety; only restrict for medical reasons |
| Obsessing over calories over quality | Prioritize nutrient density first |
| Extreme restriction → binge | Allow flexible treats; build sustainable habits |
| Skipping meals | Eat regularly to avoid energy crashes and overeating later |
| Replacing meals with supplements | Whole foods first; supplements only to fill gaps |

## Red Flags — Stop and Seek Professional Help

- Diet is used to manage a diagnosed medical condition
- Severe calorie restriction (<1200 kcal/day for adults)
- Food rules causing anxiety or social isolation
- Unexplained weight loss or gain
- Allergic reactions to foods

These cases require a registered dietitian or physician, not general guidance.

## Real-World Impact

A balanced diet reduces risk of cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, and certain cancers, while supporting consistent energy, better sleep, and stronger immunity. Small, sustainable changes compound more than short-term restrictive diets.
